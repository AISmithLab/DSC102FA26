## Overview

In this challenge, you will work on a real machine learning competition from AI Coding Gym where frontier AI agents fall short. Your goal is to outperform the AI baseline by applying your own domain judgment, iterative experimentation, and strategic use of AI as a collaborative tool.

AI gets close but not quite. You can win with careful feature engineering, hyperparameter tuning, or domain-informed preprocessing.

## Learning Loop

1. **Inspect the AI agent's work.** Download the AI agent's code and reasoning trace from the platform. Study the agent's decisions: What preprocessing did it choose? What model architecture? What did it skip or get wrong?
2. **Attempt the challenge using AI as a tool.** Work in your local coding environment with AI coding assistants. Apply your own judgment to override the AI's decisions where you see weaknesses. You may use any AI tools you like.
3. **Compare scores.** Submit your predictions on AI Coding Gym. Your score will appear on the live leaderboard alongside the AI baseline and your peers.
4. **Share work in progress in class.** We will dedicate class time for two or three students to present their approaches for 10 minutes each. Presentations are selected for methodological diversity, not top scores. Share what you tried, what worked, and what did not.
5. **Write a reflection.** Submit the structured reflection described below.

## Deliverables

1. **Challenge submission via AI Coding Gym.** Submit your predictions on the platform. You may submit as many times as you like; your best score will be recorded.
2. **AI interaction history via Canvas.** Submit the complete history of prompts, AI responses, and meaningful iterations that influenced your final solution.
3. **Final report via Canvas.** Submit a three-to-five-paragraph PDF containing:
   - **AI inspection summary:** What did the AI agent do? What were its key decisions about models, preprocessing, and features? What weaknesses did you identify in its reasoning trace?
   - **Your strategy:** What did you change and why? Describe your approach, the prompts you used with AI assistants, and the domain judgment you applied that the AI did not.
   - **Results comparison:** Compare your score with the AI baseline and include a screenshot of your leaderboard position.
   - **Reflection:** Where did the AI fall short? What did you do that it did not? What concept or skill did this teach you?

## Grading

| Component | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Challenge score from the AI Coding Gym leaderboard | 30% |
| Reflection write-up, evaluated for depth of analysis rather than only reporting scores | 40% |
| AI diagnosis, including identification of errors and weaknesses in the AI's approach | 30% |

A student who does not beat the AI baseline but writes a perceptive reflection that correctly identifies why and what concept to strengthen can still earn a strong grade. Understanding matters more than the raw score.
